## 4.5 Discussion of Findings

The evaluation results validate the relative `strong_match_margin` approach as a sound architectural decision. Gemini's 3072-dimensional text embeddings compress all real-match cosine distances into a narrow band of approximately 0.20–0.35, making any fixed absolute spine threshold either over-inclusive or over-restrictive. An earlier absolute-threshold variant produced empty spines with deceptively healthy composite scores — a failure mode the empty-spine guardrail, which forces the composite to 0.0 when every training event yields no library contribution, was designed to prevent. At the optimized value of 0.10, the relative margin self-adjusts to each query's local distance distribution, providing empirical support for this design choice over its absolute predecessor.

The 0.1155 train–holdout gap — between the best training composite of 0.8027 and the holdout composite of 0.6872 — indicates mild prompt specialization from five Phase 2 hill-climbing iterations over only eight training events, exceeding the 0.10 overfitting warning threshold. In production, where event descriptions may span a broader range of cultural contexts and genre vocabularies, this specialization risks systematic underperformance on atypical queries. Expanding the training event corpus and reducing the number of optimization iterations are the primary mitigations, forcing each prompt mutation to generalize across varied vocabulary rather than adapt to a small fixed set.

The NIM Llama-3.3-70b alignment judge provides the only metric in the evaluation framework capable of capturing subjective playlist quality rather than pipeline execution correctness. Mechanical metrics such as `acceptance_rate` and `retrieval_relevance` assess structural validity but cannot determine whether a tracklist actually fits the stated event vibe. However, a single large-language-model judge introduces genre-correlated bias: models may assign systematically higher coherence scores to certain genre or cultural contexts, potentially disadvantaging playlists with non-Western repertoire or unconventional combinations. Fully mitigating this bias would require a multi-judge ensemble or calibration against real listener ratings.

The production `overprovision_factor` of 2.0 was calibrated to the observed failure characteristics of the College/Ollama gemma3:12b model. The stub Spotify validator deterministically rejects approximately 30 percent of wildcard candidates; combined with the College model's lower reliability and the five-request-per-minute rate limit on the College server, a lower overprovision factor consistently resulted in playlists below the target size after the LangGraph retry loop exhausted its maximum three regeneration attempts. This calibration demonstrates that the overprovision factor is a provider-dependent parameter: configurations using more reliable generation models would likely require a lower value to achieve equivalent playlist-size fulfillment.

Two structural gaps emerged from the development process. First, optimized prompt files written to `eval/optimized/` require manual promotion to `app/prompts/` before influencing the live inference path, risking divergence between the evaluated and deployed configurations. Second, while both the evaluation harness and production employ cosine distance, the assumption that thresholds calibrated against in-memory ChromaDB transfer equivalently to pgvector has not been validated empirically. Manual testing also revealed a tendency to over-rely on top-ranked songs and the listening history of dominant participants, producing playlists that favoured a subset of the group. In hindsight, deeper investment in algorithmic design from the outset would have been the most impactful change.
